---
title: "Recording the uke with a $40 lavalier"
date: 2025-09-18
summary: "Condenser mics are overkill for a sitting-room recording. A cheap lav clipped to the headstock gets 80% of the way there."
---

I spent a long time convincing myself I needed a large-diaphragm condenser, a proper interface, and an acoustically-treated corner. Then I recorded a passable demo with a Rode smartLav+ clipped to the headstock of my soprano and my phone in aeroplane mode on a book.

Here's what I've learned after twenty or thirty takes that way.

## Placement

- **Clip the lav to the headstock**, not the body. The headstock is quieter (less boom) and the mic ends up roughly a foot from the soundhole.
- **Angle it across the strings**, not towards the soundhole. Pointing straight at the hole exaggerates low-mid honk.
- **Slack the cable.** If the cable is taut it transmits finger noise up the fretting hand like a telegraph.

## The phone

An iPhone's voice-memo app records 16-bit / 44.1 kHz mono, which is fine. The bigger wins:

- Turn on **aeroplane mode**. No notifications, no LTE buzz.
- Record a ten-second room-tone clip at the start of every session. It makes noise reduction in post much more forgiving.
- **Never let the phone touch the floor** — footsteps will end up in the take.

## Post

I use Audacity. Two moves cover most of it:

1. **Noise reduction** sampled from that room-tone clip (12 dB, sensitivity 6, frequency smoothing 3).
2. A gentle **high-pass at 80 Hz** to get rid of the rumble the lav picks up.

That's it. No compression, no EQ beyond the high-pass. A recording made this way won't get you on the radio, but it'll happily sit on a blog post next to the chord chart, and that's what I wanted.
